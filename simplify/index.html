SEC. 01

What Enoch does

Your records, with proof nobody changed them

Every day your firm creates records that matter: client files, financial records, approvals, correspondence. Most live in systems where someone with access could quietly change them, and you would never know. Enoch gives you a way to prove, months or years later, that a record is exactly what it was when you sealed it.

Built for Canadian regulated firms: law practices answering to the Law Society, clinics handling PHIPA-protected health records, accountants preparing CRA-facing workpapers.

FIG. 01 · A timestamp in a database is just a claim.
A sealed fingerprint is evidence.

01

Databases can be rewritten

A timestamp in a normal system only shows what the system claims happened. It does not prove the record stayed the same.

02

Proof is scattered

Records live across different tools. Enoch ties what you choose to seal into one checkable timeline.

03

Checking is expensive

Audits and disputes mean manual work and specialists. A sealed fingerprint lets anyone check a record in seconds.

SEC. 02

How it works

Three steps, no trust required

Enoch works alongside the systems you already use. It does not replace them.

STEP 01 · CAPTURE YOUR RECORD STAYS WITH YOU

Capture

Connectors linked to your existing systems capture each record you choose into your private record system, inside your own environment. You decide what gets captured.

STEP 02 · SEAL ONLY THE FINGERPRINT GOES PUBLIC

Seal

Enoch makes a digital fingerprint of the record and posts it to the public ledger with a timestamp, signed with your key. Your record contents, filenames, and personal data stay private and are never sent.

STEP 03 · VERIFY MATCH OR NO MATCH

Verify

To check a record, anyone recomputes its fingerprint and compares it to the one on the public ledger. A match means untouched. No Enoch account needed, and checking never depends on us staying in business.

THE CORE PROMISE

Your records stay in systems you control. The signing keys stay with you too, and Enoch cannot create or sign records for you.

SEC. 04

Honest limitations

What Enoch does not prove

Sealing narrows the trust problem. It does not remove every source of error or fraud.

LIMIT / 01

Truthfulness

Enoch proves a record existed in a certain state at a certain time. It does not prove the content was true.

LIMIT / 02

Completeness

Enoch proves the integrity of records it receives. It cannot prove every relevant event was sent in.

LIMIT / 03

Depends on one network

Your private system runs on Hedera, and fingerprints go to the Hedera public ledger. If the public ledger goes down, records queue up locally and get sealed after recovery.

LIMIT / 04

Not yet proven

Enoch has not launched. No independent security review is done, no court has accepted Enoch proof, and no regulator has endorsed it.

SEC. 05

Questions and direct answers

Questions, answered plainly

Straight answers, no jargon. If your question is not here, write to us.

What will Enoch cost?

Pricing is not final and will be published before launch. The Community Version will be free and open source, so running it yourself will have no license fee. Pilot terms, including any cost, are agreed with each firm in writing before the pilot starts.

Will it work with the software I already use?

That is the plan, but no integrations exist yet. We build a connector for each system, and we will only list integrations here once they are built and working, not as promises.

Who sees my data?

Only people you already allow. Your records stay in systems you control, and our people cannot access them. Only digital fingerprints go public, and those reveal nothing about your records.

What if I lose a key?

The signing keys stay with you, which means losing one is serious. Key recovery and replacement procedures are worked out with each firm in the pilot agreement, before anything starts.

Will a court accept this?

No court has weighed in on Enoch proof yet, and we claim no legal acceptance. Public timestamps are final within seconds, but their legal weight varies by place. Your own lawyer should review this for your situation.

How long does proof last?

Fingerprints stay on the public ledger permanently. As long as the ledger exists, anyone can check them, with or without us.

What is Hedera?

Hedera is a public digital ledger network, run by a council of global organizations. Enoch uses it in two ways: your private record system runs on it inside your environment, and fingerprints are posted to its public ledger with a timestamp.

Do I need cryptocurrency? What is Hedera?

Hedera is a public blockchain network, a shared digital ledger that no single company controls. It has its own cryptocurrency called HBAR, which pays a tiny fee (a fraction of a cent) each time we record a fingerprint.

You never need to buy, hold, or think about cryptocurrency. We cover all network fees during your pilot. If you continue afterward, your agreement will spell out who pays. There is no wallet to set up and nothing to manage.

We use a public network because the proof needs to live somewhere independent. If we kept it in our own database, you would have to trust us. On a public ledger, anyone can check the proof without trusting anyone.

What if someone leaves or we lose a key?

We set things up so no single person holds all the power. If someone leaves, their access is removed and replaced. If a key is lost, the remaining key holders can still sign, and we help you create a replacement. Your old proofs keep working.

What happens if a check fails?

You will see a clear NO MATCH with both fingerprints shown side by side. This means the file changed since it was recorded, even slightly. Often it is innocent, like re-saving a file or converting formats. Nothing is automatically reported to anyone outside your firm. Your team checks what changed using the history Enoch keeps.

Can anyone figure out what I recorded?

No. The public fingerprint cannot be turned back into your document. But two things are visible: when you recorded something (the timestamp), and, if your document is very short and guessable like the word "approved," someone who guesses correctly could confirm it. We recommend including enough detail in each record to make guessing impractical.

Should I record everything or just some things?

That is your call, but be consistent. Enoch proves what you recorded. It cannot prove what you did not. If you only record some things, someone could later ask why. We suggest writing down a simple rule for what gets recorded and sticking to it.

Is there a gap between when something happens and when it is protected?

Yes. There is a short window, usually minutes, between when Enoch captures an event and when its fingerprint reaches the public ledger. During that window, someone with deep access to your systems could theoretically change the record before it is sealed. We are upfront about this because it matters. Once anchored, the record is tamper-evident.

What happens if you go out of business?

Your records stay with you, and old fingerprints remain checkable on the public ledger without us.

Can I delete things?

Yes. You can delete your local records under your own retention rules. The public fingerprint stays, but it reveals nothing about the record.

What if a record changes?

A correction becomes a new record that replaces the old one, sealed separately. The original stays visible, so the full history is preserved. If a record changes without a proper correction, the fingerprint will not match, which is exactly what the system is designed to catch.

How do I verify a record?

Recompute the record's fingerprint with standard tools and compare it to the fingerprint on the public ledger. A match means the record is exactly the same as when it was sealed. No Enoch account or permission is needed.

What exactly goes on the public ledger?

Only a digital fingerprint made from the record, plus a timestamp. Your document contents, filenames, and personal data never go on the ledger.
