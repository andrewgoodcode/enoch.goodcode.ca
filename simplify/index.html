SEC. 01

The integrity gap

Audit trails should prove what happened

Businesses like yours create important records every day. Most are stored in systems where someone with access, a software update, or a database change can quietly rewrite history.

FIG. 01 · A timestamp in a database is just a claim.

During audits, investigations, or disputes, proving records are intact often takes manual work and expensive specialists.

SEC. 03

How it works

From your records to proof anyone can check

Enoch is a system, not a single app: your own private record system, connectors that link it to the public ledger and to the systems you already use, and a verification dashboard. It does not replace your existing systems.

BLOCK 01 · CAPTURE INPUT: YOUR RECORD + STANDARD LABEL

Capture

Connectors linked to your existing systems capture each record along with a standard label (record ID, event type, and time) into your private record system, inside your own environment. You decide which events get captured.

label → record ID · event type · time captured

BLOCK 02 · ANCHOR OUTPUT: FINGERPRINT + PUBLIC TIMESTAMP

Seal

A connector sends a digital fingerprint from your private system to the public ledger, signed with your key. The public ledger stamps it with a public timestamp. Only the fingerprint crosses over: your record contents, filenames, and personal data stay private and are never sent.

fingerprint 01ae732d…57187284 · signed by your key · your records never leave your systems

BLOCK 03 · VERIFY RESULT: MATCH OR NO MATCH, NO MIDDLEMAN

Verify

The proof package contains the original record, its label, the fingerprint method, the sealed fingerprint, who signed it, and the public timestamp. To check a record, anyone recomputes the fingerprint and compares it to the one on the public ledger. No Enoch account needed.

recompute → compare → exact match

ARCHITECTURE PRINCIPLE

Your records stay in systems you control. The signing keys stay with you too, and Enoch cannot create or sign records for you. Your private system runs in your environment: on your own servers, in cloud accounts you control, or on supplied hardware for onsite setups. Connectors link it to the public ledger and connect your existing systems to your private network. The public ledger receives only the fingerprint needed to check integrity.

SEC. 04

Trust and proof model

What the proof establishes

An exact match proves a record is exactly the same, down to the last character, as when its fingerprint was recorded at the public timestamp. It does not prove the record was truthful.

KEYS / YOU HOLD THEM

Your keys

The signing keys stay with you. Enoch cannot secretly create, change, or sign your records.

CORRECTIONS / NEW VERSIONS

Corrections stay visible

A correction is a new record that replaces the old one. The original seal stays, and the full history remains visible.

CHECKING / INDEPENDENT

Anyone can check

Every proof package carries what you need to recompute the fingerprint and find it on the public ledger. Checking does not depend on Enoch staying in business.

METHOD / UPGRADEABLE

Future-proof method

Enoch uses an industry-standard fingerprint method today. Records can be re-sealed with a stronger method later without losing the original timestamp.

SCOPE / WHAT YOU SEND

Only what you send

Enoch seals what your systems send to your private system. It cannot prove an event happened if it was never sent in.

DELETION / PRIVATE ERASURE

Delete without exposing

You can delete your local records under your own retention rules. The fingerprint remains but reveals nothing about the record.

SEC. 08

Honest limitations

What Enoch does not prove

Sealing narrows the trust problem. It does not remove every source of error or fraud.

LIMIT / 01

Truthfulness

Enoch proves a record existed in a certain state at a certain time. It does not prove the content was true. A way to flag disputed records is planned.

LIMIT / 02

Completeness

Enoch proves the integrity of records it receives. It cannot prove every relevant event was sent in.

LIMIT / 03

Depends on one network

Enoch depends on Hedera twice: your private system runs on it, and the public ledger is where fingerprints go. If the public ledger goes down, records queue up locally and get sealed after recovery. The gap before sealing is real and will be documented.

LIMIT / 04

Not yet proven

Enoch has not launched. No independent security review is done, no court has accepted Enoch proof, and no regulator has endorsed it.

How this differs

REGULAR LOGS

A regular log can be changed by someone with enough access. A sealed fingerprint makes any later change detectable.

WRITE-ONCE STORAGE

Write-once storage proves integrity to people who trust the company running it. Enoch lets outsiders check against a public ledger without trusting us.

STANDARD TIMESTAMPS

A standard digital timestamp relies on one authority. Hedera timestamps come from the whole network agreeing. Their legal weight still varies by place.

ATRUST AND PROOF

What exactly goes on the public ledger?

A digital fingerprint made from the record plus a standard label (record ID, event type, time). Connectors send that fingerprint from your private system to the public ledger. Your document contents, filenames, and personal data stay private and never go on the ledger.

What does a successful check prove?

It proves the record you supplied is exactly the same, down to the last character, as the record sealed at the public timestamp. It does not prove the content was truthful, or prove who wrote it beyond who held the signing key.

Who controls the signing keys?

You do. Enoch cannot create or sign records for you. This stops anyone at the vendor from secretly making up a record and stamping it as genuine.

How are corrections handled?

A correction becomes a new record that replaces the old one, sealed separately. The original stays visible and the full history is preserved.

Can Enoch prove that every event was logged?

No. You decide what your systems send to your private system, and Enoch seals what it receives. It cannot prove that events never sent in happened.

How can an auditor check a record?

The proof package includes the original record, its label, the fingerprint method, the sealed fingerprint, who signed it, and the public timestamp. The auditor recomputes the fingerprint with standard tools, then finds the matching entry on the public ledger. No Enoch account or permission is needed.

What if the fingerprint method becomes outdated?

Records can be re-sealed with a stronger method while keeping the original seal and its timestamp.

BDATA CONTROL AND EXIT

Where do our records live?

In systems you control. Enoch is designed not to hold your record contents, so our people cannot access them.

What happens if we delete a record?

You can delete your local copy under your own retention rules. The public fingerprint stays, but it reveals nothing about the record.

What happens if The Good Code Company fails or is acquired?

Your records stay with you. Old fingerprints remain checkable on the public ledger without us.

What could Enoch hand over if ordered by a court?

Enoch runs inside your environment and is designed to hold none of your record contents on our systems. So there is no record content for us to hand over. Our policies on operational data will be documented before launch.

Can we take our data and leave?

Yes, anytime. Old fingerprints keep working on the public ledger after you leave.
