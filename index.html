SEC. 01

The integrity gap

Audit trails should prove what happened

Regulated organizations generate critical records every day. Most are still stored in systems where administrators, integrations, or database changes can quietly rewrite history.

FIG. 01 · A timestamp inside a database is a claim.
An anchored hash is evidence.

01

Mutable records create doubt

A timestamp in a conventional database shows what the system says happened. It does not prove the record remained unchanged.

02

Evidence is scattered

Events, approvals, files, and document revisions live across separate tools, making one defensible timeline difficult to assemble.

03

Verification is expensive

During audits, investigations, or disputes, proving integrity often requires manual reconciliation and specialist review.

SEC. 02

How it works

From operational record to verifiable evidence

Enoch is a system, not a single application: a private Hedera instance, modular microservices that bridge it to the public mainnet and connect it to the customer's existing systems, and a verification dashboard. It does not replace the system of record.

BLOCK 01 · CAPTURE INPUT: RECORD BYTES + METADATA ENVELOPE

Capture

Microservices connected to the customer's existing systems capture record bytes and a canonical metadata envelope containing the record ID, event type, and capture timestamp into the customer's private Hedera instance, inside their own environment. The customer decides which events are captured.

envelope → record_id · event_type · capture_timestamp

BLOCK 02 · ANCHOR OUTPUT: DIGEST + CONSENSUS TIMESTAMP

Anchor

A bridge microservice submits a SHA-256 hash from the private instance to the public mainnet, signed with the customer's key. Hedera Consensus Service assigns a network consensus timestamp. Only the hash crosses from private to public: record contents, filenames, and personal data stay on the private side and are not submitted.

digest 01ae732d…57187284 · signed by customer key · contents never leave customer systems

BLOCK 03 · VERIFY RESULT: MATCH OR MISMATCH, NO INTERMEDIARY

Verify

The evidence export contains the original record bytes, associated metadata, canonicalization specification, hash algorithm, anchored digest, signer account and public-key data, plus the Hedera network, topic, sequence number, and consensus timestamp. A verifier recomputes the hash and checks the referenced message through a compatible mirror node that retains it. No Enoch account or service is required.

recompute → compare → cryptographic match

ARCHITECTURE PRINCIPLE

Records stay in customer-controlled systems. Anchoring keys also stay with the customer, and Enoch cannot create or sign records on a customer's behalf. The private instance runs in the customer's environment: on their own infrastructure, in cloud infrastructure they control, or on supplied server hardware for onsite nodes. Modular microservices bridge it to the public mainnet and connect the customer's existing systems to their private network. The public ledger receives only the proof required to test integrity.

SEC. 03

Trust and proof model

What the proof establishes

A cryptographic match establishes that a record is byte-identical to the record anchored at the stated consensus time. It does not establish that the record was truthful.

KEYS / HELD BY CUSTOMER

Customer-held keys

Anchoring keys remain with the customer. Enoch cannot silently create, alter, or sign a customer's record.

CORRECTIONS / SUPERSEDING

Visible corrections

A correction is a new, superseding record. The original anchor remains and the version chain shows the complete history.

VERIFICATION / INDEPENDENT

Independent verification

Every evidence export is designed to carry the inputs and receipt needed to reproduce the digest and locate its exact Hedera message. Verification uses a compatible mirror node that retains the referenced message. It does not depend on Enoch remaining available.

ALGORITHM / SHA-256 TODAY

Hash agility

The design uses SHA-256 today. Records can be re-anchored under a stronger algorithm later without removing the original timestamp.

SCOPE / SELECTIVE

Selective by definition

Enoch anchors what the customer's existing systems send to their private instance. It cannot prove that an event never submitted occurred, or that the log is complete.

DELETION / ORPHAN PROOF

Deletion without disclosure

A customer can delete local records under its retention or expungement policy. The anchor remains as an orphan proof that reveals no record content.

SEC. 06

Honest limitations

What Enoch does not prove

Anchoring narrows the trust problem. It does not remove every source of error, omission, or fraud.

LIMIT / 01

Truthfulness

Enoch proves that a record existed in a specific state at a specific time. It does not prove that the content was true. A contestation marker is part of the design.

LIMIT / 02

Completeness

Enoch proves the integrity of records it receives. It cannot prove that every relevant event was submitted.

LIMIT / 03

Single-network dependency

Enoch depends on Hedera twice over: the private instance, and the public mainnet it anchors to. During a mainnet outage, events queue locally and anchor after recovery. The pre-anchor window is real and will be documented.

LIMIT / 04

Unverified claims

Enoch is pre-launch. No independent security audit has been completed, no court has admitted Enoch-verified evidence, and no regulator has endorsed it.

How the proof model differs

CONVENTIONAL LOGS

A conventional log can be changed by someone with sufficient access. An anchored hash makes a later change detectable.

WORM STORAGE

A WORM appliance proves integrity to parties that trust its operator. Enoch is designed for third-party verification against a public ledger without trusting the vendor.

RFC 3161

An RFC 3161 timestamp relies on one timestamp authority. Hedera timestamps are produced through network consensus. Their legal authority still varies by jurisdiction.

SEC. 08

ATRUST AND PROOF

What exactly goes on Hedera?

A SHA-256 hash computed over a canonical package containing the record bytes and a metadata envelope with the record ID, event type, and capture timestamp. The bridge microservices submit that hash from the customer's private instance to the public mainnet. Document contents, filenames, and personal data stay on the private side and do not go on the ledger.

What does a successful verification prove?

It proves that the supplied record is byte-identical to the record anchored at the Hedera consensus time. It does not prove that the content was truthful or establish authorship beyond custody of the signing key.

Who controls the anchoring keys?

The customer. Enoch cannot create or sign records on a customer's behalf. This prevents a vendor insider from silently fabricating a customer record and stamping it as genuine.

How are corrections handled?

A correction becomes a new, superseding record that is anchored separately. The original remains visible and the version chain preserves the complete history.

Can Enoch prove that every event was logged?

No. The customer defines what their existing systems send to the private instance, and Enoch anchors what that instance receives. It cannot prove the completeness of events that were never submitted.

How can an auditor verify a record?

The evidence export is designed to include the original record bytes, associated metadata, canonicalization specification, hash algorithm, anchored digest, signer account and public-key data, Hedera network, HCS topic, message sequence number, and consensus timestamp. The auditor recomputes the digest with standard tooling or the published verifier, then checks the referenced message through a compatible mirror node that retains it. No Enoch account, permission, or service dependency is required.

What happens if SHA-256 becomes unsuitable?

The design supports re-anchoring under a stronger algorithm while preserving the original anchor and its timestamp.

BDATA CONTROL AND EXIT

Where do customer records live?

In customer-controlled systems. Enoch is designed not to hold record contents, so its employees and contractors cannot access them.

What happens if a customer deletes a record?

The local record can be deleted under the customer's retention or expungement policy. The public anchor remains as an orphan proof that something existed at a time, but it reveals no content.

What happens if The Good Code Company fails or is acquired?

Customer records remain with the customer. Old anchors remain verifiable through the public ledger without Enoch or The Good Code Company.

What could Enoch provide under subpoena?

Enoch runs inside the customer's environment and is designed to hold no customer record contents on any vendor system. It therefore has no record content to produce. Operational and account metadata policies will be documented before launch.

Can customers export and leave?

The design permits export at any time. Verification of old anchors continues through the public ledger after exit.

CIMPLEMENTATION AND OPERATIONS

What does an Enoch deployment consist of?

A private Hedera instance running in the customer's environment, modular microservices that bridge it to the public mainnet and connect the customer's existing systems to their private network, and a verification dashboard. Where the customer wants onsite server nodes for the private network, the hardware can be included. It connects to existing systems rather than replacing them.

Which integrations are available?

None have shipped. Integrations will be listed here only after they ship, not as roadmap promises.

How long will implementation take?

Each deployment is customized: the bridge microservices and the connections to existing systems are fitted to the customer's environment. No firm implementation timeline is claimed before pilots produce evidence.

Events queue locally and anchor after recovery. The interval before anchoring is a real exposure window and will be documented in the system's operating guidance.

What administration is required?

Role-based access is planned and keys stay with the customer. Avoiding dedicated Enoch headcount is a design goal, not a proven result.
