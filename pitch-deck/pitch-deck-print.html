SEC. 02 — Problem

Regulated firms live and die by record integrity.

Law firms, medical practices, accountants, notaries, and NGOs face audits, disputes, and compliance reviews where they must prove records are complete and unaltered.

  • Enterprise content systems are built and priced for large corporations
  • Manual processes (printed logs, spreadsheets, email archives) cannot prove a record was never changed
  • Generic cloud storage offers no tamper-evidence guarantees

When a regulator, court, or client asks “prove this record is authentic,” small firms have no affordable answer.

SEC. 03 — Solution

Enoch: Trust as a Service. Private data, public proof.

Tamper-evident event logging and documentation archiving for regulated enterprises.

  • Customer events and documents stay private in their own systems
  • Cryptographic hashes and trusted timestamps anchor to the Hedera public network via the Hedera Consensus Service
  • Anyone can independently verify a record was never altered, without seeing its contents

No new database to migrate to. No records leave customer control. Just proof.

SEC. 04 — How it works

Seven stages, one trust boundary.

  1. Sources. Eight types feed the system: Staff, Associates, Management, Stakeholders (customers, clients, members), Internal systems, External systems, Documentation, Devices. Most flow both ways.
  2. Bridge modules. Custom connectors per source type, drawn from an ever-growing module library. Companies use only what they need.
  3. Classification. Auto-recognition plus customer policy routes each item to archive, available data, or event log.
  4. Private Hedera. Each customer gets a private, permissioned Hedera instance. Contents stay inside the customer boundary, customer-controlled.
  5. Attestation. Intelligent batching (time plus volume plus priority) submits hashes to the public ledger. Only proofs cross, never contents.
  6. Verify. Browser-based verifier checks any record against the public anchor. Match or no match, no trust in Enoch required.
  7. Key custody. Multi-key with multi-sig (M-of-N), customer-held.

SEC. 06 — Editions

Two ways to run Enoch.

Pilot now. The Enoch team sets up the private instance, builds the integrations, deploys in the customer environment, and manages it day to day under SLA. For firms that want it handled.

Community Version

Upcoming. The same core system, published as open source. Completely self-managed, no SLA. For developers, researchers, and firms with in-house capability.

SEC. 07 — Market

The wedge is small firms with acute audit pain.

  • Law firms (5–50 lawyers), medical and dental practices, accounting firms
  • Notaries, NGOs, municipal government, construction, insurance

Expansion: upmarket to mid-market enterprise and government once the wedge is proven.

Why Hedera wins here: every anchored record is a Consensus Service transaction. Enoch’s growth is Hedera’s transaction growth. Small-firm penetration on Hedera is near zero today.

SEC. 08 — Business model

Contracts for the handled, open source for the capable.

  • Enterprise: annual contracts per deployment. Pilots convert to production subscriptions covering setup, integration, management, and SLA support.
  • Community: open source, self-managed, no license fee. Drives adoption, developer mindshare, and Hedera transaction volume. Enterprise upsell path for firms that outgrow self-management.

Pricing calibrated to small-firm budgets, not enterprise seat licenses. Unit economics to be validated in pilots.

SEC. 10 — Why now

Four forces, one window.

  1. Regulatory pressure is increasing. Data integrity requirements tighten across legal, medical, and financial sectors.
  2. Hedera is enterprise-ready. The Consensus Service is the trust anchor; Enoch makes it usable without blockchain expertise.
  3. The small-firm gap is open. Enterprise vendors price out the firms that need this most.
  4. Open source is the wedge. A community edition builds trust in a product whose value proposition is trust.
